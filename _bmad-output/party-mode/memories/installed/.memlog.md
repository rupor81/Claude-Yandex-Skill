- (decision) Epic decomposition: variant 2 (per connector) adopted - Calendar whole, then Mail whole, then Disk whole
- (decision) Condition from Winston: cross-cutting machinery (risk registry, dry_run, error envelope) must be designed in yandex_core against all three protocols, not shaped by whichever connector is built first. Goes into acceptance criteria
- (decision) Condition from Sally: within each epic, read stories precede write stories. The read-before-write ordering was dropped at epic level, not abandoned
- (event) Mary's finding: the PRD's read-first rationale predates the spine's five safety mechanisms (destructiveHint, client approval, trash-only, mandatory scope, dry_run) and was already paid for by the architecture
- (event) Sally's finding, the strongest of the session: a partial connector misrepresents its own capability surface to the model - Claude sees calendar read tools, is asked to reschedule, finds no tool and reports success anyway. Same disease as NFR3, one level up
- (event) User's own argument (file churn) judged the weakest - adding write tools is additive and does not rewrite read code
- (override) REVERSAL of yesterday's party condition: the Epic 1 core is NOT required to be designed against all three protocols. That condition was unverifiable with only CalDAV in hand. Replaced by an explicit Epic 2 story that revisits the core once IMAP reveals the real commonality
- (decision) Acceptance scenario becomes the last story of Epic 2 rather than an epic or an implicit outcome - it belongs to neither epic and would otherwise go unverified
- (decision) Epic 3's dependency on Epic 2 for OAuth recorded explicitly as deliberate, not accidental
- (event) Morphological analysis surfaced a cut along authentication mechanism (Calendar alone vs Mail+Disk together) - the only split matching the system's real coupling. Rejected: twenty-tool epic, dissolved connector boundaries. Kept the insight that the Epic 2 to Epic 3 OAuth dependency is real
